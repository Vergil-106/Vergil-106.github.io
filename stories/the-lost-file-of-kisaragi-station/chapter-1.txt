Chapter 1: Curiosity kills the Cat..

My name is Ethan Wilder. I’m a retired blogger, now about 60 years old.

When I was a student, around 38 years ago, I traveled to Japan quite often. I made several friends during those trips, most of them teenagers like me. I’d only recently started blogging, and I was already completely hooked.

One day, my friends and I decided to go exploring. An abandoned place, naturally! We were teenagers, and even though I was the oldest of the bunch, they insisted I come along. In the end, I gave in. We chose a place with a dark reputation: Inunaki Tunnel.

Local folklore spoke of an old telephone booth somewhere nearby. The story went that at exactly 1 AM, a call would come in from Inunaki Village. Answer it, and you’d supposedly open a gateway to the village itself.

We eventually set off for Inunaki Dam. By evening, we’d reached the infamous telephone booth. We were getting a little bored, so we decided to explore the area. After wandering around for a while, we headed toward the dam.

Here’s a little background on the layout. It was summer, and the small river nearby had almost dried up. A dam had also been built close to the tunnel, so the dry riverbed seemed like the easiest way to get there.

When we reached the tunnel entrance, something caught our eye: several large wooden planks had been nailed across the whole entrance. There was, however, a small gap between them—just wide enough to squeeze through. So, naturally, we climbed over the planks and went inside.

There were five of us. We switched on our flashlights and started down the tunnel. It was probably about half a kilometre long. We kept walking until we finally reached the far end.

That’s when we came across something I hadn’t expected.

Near the tunnel’s exit, we spotted railway tracks. I couldn’t make sense of them. There shouldn’t have been any tracks in a place like that.

Then I noticed something lying nearby.

A mobile phone.

We tried turning it on, but it wouldn’t respond. It seemed completely dead. After looking around for a little longer, we decided to leave.

By the time we made it out of the tunnel, it was already dark—sometime around eight or nine in the evening.
